Below is an up-to-date equity research briefing on Circle Internet Group, Inc. (NYSE: CRCL) as of January 31, 2026. Circle listed on the NYSE on June 5, 2025. A number of metrics below reflect only a few quarters of public company data.

1) Company Overview - What it does: Circle issues and operates fiat-backed stablecoins (USDC, EURC) and provides a platform for on-chain money movement (Circle Payments Network), developer services (wallets, CCTP cross-chain transfers, xReserve), and is building Arc, its own enterprise-grade L1 blockchain. Circle earns primarily from interest on the assets backing USDC and secondarily from subscription/services and transaction fees. (circle.com) - Main revenue streams: - Reserve income (interest on short-dated U.S. Treasuries, overnight repos and bank cash that back USDC). - “Other revenue” (subscription/services and transaction revenue) – rapidly growing but still small versus reserve income. (circle.com) - Industry/positioning: Second-largest global stablecoin issuer by circulation; share rose to ~29% by Q3 2025 (still behind Tether). Circle emphasizes transparency (monthly attestations, BlackRock-managed Circle Reserve Fund) and compliance, and now has a U.S. federal framework (GENIUS Act) under which to grow. (circle.com) - Ticker/Listing: NYSE: CRCL; IPO priced at $31 on June 4, 2025; shares opened at $69 and traded as high as ~$103 on day one. (cnbc.com)

Versus peers: COIN P/S ~8.2; EV/S ~7.1; PYPL P/S ~1.5–1.7; EV/EBITDA ~8–8.5. CRCL screens cheaper than COIN on sales but far richer than large fintechs like PYPL on most multiples. 3) Business Quality and Moat - Advantages - Regulatory posture and trust: Monthly attestations and a BlackRock-run Circle Reserve Fund for USDC reserves bolster credibility and transparency. (blackrock.com) - Distribution/scale: USDC integrated across major exchanges, wallets, payments partners (e.g., Fiserv pilot; Visa’s USDC settlement expansion recently noted) and 28+ chains; Arc aims to deepen platform control. (investor.circle.com) - Network effects: More wallets, exchanges, and enterprises using USDC → higher balances → higher reserve income and more services uptake. (circle.com) - Constraints/risk factors within model - High rate sensitivity: 2024–2025 revenue largely from reserve yields; S‑1 quantified large earnings sensitivity to 100 bp rate moves. (sec.gov) - Distribution dependence: Significant revenue share to partners (notably Coinbase) compresses net reserve margin. (axios.com) - Competitive pressure: Tether remains dominant and is launching a U.S.-compliant USAT stablecoin under the GENIUS Act, likely intensifying competition. (coindesk.com) - Customer concentration: Revenue sharing and on‑platform balances concentrated with a small number of large partners (e.g., Coinbase). (circle.com) - Supplier/commodity exposure: Exposure to short-dated U.S. Treasuries/repo and bank deposits; reserve fund notes specify no sponsor support obligation. (blackrock.com) - Pricing power/margins: Circle cannot pay interest on stablecoins under GENIUS Act; monetization relies on reserve income and fee-based services. RLDC margin guided around ~38% for 2025. (circle.com)

6) Major Downside Risks - Interest-rate cuts: Material earnings sensitivity to lower short rates; street commentary repeatedly flags this risk. (barrons.com) - Competition: Tether’s USAT (U.S.-compliant) launch could pressure USDC share and economics; other fintechs (PayPal USD, Fiserv’s FIUSD) increase fragmentation. (coindesk.com) - Regulatory/political: GENIUS Act implementation may evolve; bank-like requirements, audits, and AML/BSA obligations add cost/complexity. (gtlaw.com) - Distribution cost pressure: Coinbase/on‑platform economics may keep net reserve margin constrained. (businesswire.com) - Execution risk: Arc L1 and CPN adoption, cross-chain infrastructure (Interop Labs integration) and monetization timelines. (circle.com) - Liquidity/market events: A shock to reserves or a redemption wave could stress operations despite protections; history shows stablecoin runs are possible sector‑wide. (circle.com)
